# Why “Someone I Follow Posted About It” Beats Any Slogan

## Introduction

People have always trusted recommendations from people they know. Digital platforms have extended that behaviour: a post from a friend, community leader, specialist or creator can feel more believable than a polished brand message.

This is social proof. It does not replace good advertising, but it can make an unfamiliar business feel safer, more relevant and easier to try.

## Why peer signals matter

A slogan is controlled by the business, so customers naturally treat it with caution. A recommendation appears to come from someone with an independent view. When that person has credibility with the audience, the recommendation reduces the perceived risk of trying something new.

The key word is credibility. A large following alone is not proof that a recommendation will matter. Relevance and trust are more important than reach.

## Choose the right advocates

Look beyond celebrities and large influencers. Customers, local experts, staff members, community organisers and niche creators can all be effective advocates. Choose people whose audience overlaps with the customers you want, and whose style fits your brand.

Ask whether the partnership makes sense without payment. If the answer is no, the endorsement may feel forced. The best collaborations usually begin with a real product fit or shared interest.

## Example

A new meal-prep service wants to reach busy parents. A local parent who regularly shares practical school-week routines may be a stronger partner than a national lifestyle personality. A useful post could show how the meals fit into a hectic evening, explain what was ordered and share an honest observation.

The content is effective because it demonstrates use in context. It gives the audience information they can apply to their own lives.

## Give people something worth sharing

Social proof cannot be manufactured from nothing. The product, experience and customer service must first be good enough to generate genuine enthusiasm. Make sharing easy by providing a clear referral link, a photogenic but authentic experience, useful information or a reason for customers to tell others.

Do not script every word. Give partners accurate facts, key messages and disclosure guidance, then allow their own voice to come through. Overly controlled content often feels like an advert because it is one.

## Use testimonials responsibly

Always obtain permission before publishing a customer’s words or image. Do not edit comments in ways that change their meaning, invent reviews or hide required disclosures for paid partnerships. Trust is difficult to win and easy to lose.

Where a result is unusual, add context. A responsible business avoids implying that every customer will receive exactly the same outcome.

## Practical steps

- Identify five customers or community voices who already align with your audience.
- Ask recent happy customers for a specific, honest review.
- Create a referral experience that takes less than a minute to share.
- Test a small collaboration with a relevant local or niche creator.
- Track quality outcomes: enquiries, redemptions, bookings and repeat purchases.

## Conclusion

People pay attention to people. When a trusted voice shares a useful, honest experience, it can make a business more credible than the cleverest slogan. Focus on genuine relationships and a service worth recommending.

**CTA:** Contact one satisfied customer or aligned local creator this week and ask whether they would be willing to share an honest experience with your business.
