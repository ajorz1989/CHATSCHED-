# Stop Trying to Pull Customers In. Show Up Where They Already Are

## Introduction

Businesses often expect customers to change their routines: visit a new website, download another app, follow a new account or learn a complicated booking process. Every extra step creates friction.

A better approach is to meet customers in the places and habits they already use. When a business fits naturally into an existing routine, taking the next step feels easier.

## Understand customer habits

Start by observing how customers solve the problem before they find you. Do they ask friends in community groups? Search maps? Use WhatsApp? Compare reviews? Visit a local market? The answer should guide where and how you show up.

Do not choose channels only because they are popular. Choose them because your customers already use them at the moment a need appears.

## Reduce friction

Friction is anything that makes action harder than it needs to be: unclear prices, slow replies, long forms, broken links or instructions that require too much effort. Small improvements can have a large impact because they remove reasons to postpone.

For example, if customers typically enquire by message, make the first reply useful. Include availability, a price range, the next step and a simple way to book rather than sending them through several pages first.

## Example

A mobile car-wash service notices customers often ask for recommendations in neighbourhood WhatsApp groups. Instead of only posting general social media ads, the business could build relationships with group administrators, share a clear service menu when appropriate and offer an easy booking link that works well on a phone.

The service is not forcing people to adopt a new behaviour. It is becoming accessible within a behaviour that already exists.

## Match the format to the channel

Each channel has its own expectations. Short videos may work for demonstrations. Search listings work for urgent needs. Email works for follow-up and retention. Messaging works for quick questions and appointments. In-person events work for trust-building and sampling.

Adapt the message without losing the core value. A customer should recognise the same business whether they find it on a map listing, an email or a community recommendation.

## Make the next action obvious

When a customer is ready, do not make them guess what to do. Use clear calls to action: “Book a collection slot,” “Ask for today’s availability,” “Get a quote,” or “View the menu.” One primary action per message is usually enough.

Test the experience yourself on a phone. If you cannot complete the journey quickly, neither will many potential customers.

## Practical steps

1. Ask ten recent customers where they first looked for a solution.
2. Identify the two channels that appear most often.
3. Simplify the first action in each channel to one clear step.
4. Prepare fast, helpful responses for common questions.
5. Review where enquiries drop off and remove the biggest obstacle.

## Conclusion

Marketing becomes more effective when it respects the customer’s existing habits. Instead of constantly trying to pull people into your world, make it simple for them to find and choose you in theirs.
